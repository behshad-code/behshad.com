EXECUTIVE CASE STUDY / GLOBAL REVENUE & SERVICE TRANSFORMATION

Transforming the Revenue Engine

Global Order-to-Cash & Service Operations Transformation

As CIO following Verifone's 2018 take-private, I led a multiyear effort to connect an acquisition-built business across customer acquisition, orders, merchant activation, fulfillment, billing, collections and support.

The work was about how the business operated, not just the applications it used. I focused on common processes, customer experience, accountable ownership and coordinated technology modernization, with the revenue-to-cash cycle at the center.

01 THE INHERITED PROBLEM

ACQUISITION-BUILT COMPLEXITY

The customer saw one company. The operating model often behaved like many.

Years of acquisitions had accumulated regional workflows, separate applications and local business practices without equivalent end-to-end integration.

A sale could be captured in one system, turned into a contract in another, fulfilled through regional operations, provisioned through specialized platforms and billed by a separate finance process. Every handoff created another opportunity for incomplete data, rework or delayed service activation.

The underlying issue was broader than technology debt. Different countries and legal entities made their own decisions about products, pricing, customer records, invoicing and service support. Some differences were legally necessary. Others reflected the history of the acquired businesses.

My starting point was to understand those dependencies as a single operating problem, then connect the business case for change to commercial execution, financial control and customer experience.

THE BUSINESS QUESTION

What happens between signing a customer and receiving accurate, collectible cash for a service that actually works?

01 / REVENUE

Speed to cash

Order defects and missed activation or billing triggers could postpone the moment a contract became a billable, collectible service.

02 / ECONOMICS

Cost to serve

Manual reentry, fragmented support and local workarounds increased operational effort and made consistent scale harder.

03 / CONTROL

Financial visibility

Unreconciled customer, contract, product and payment records complicated reporting, assurance and accountable decision-making.

02 THE CONNECTED OPERATING MODEL

SEVEN STAGES / ONE CUSTOMER-TO-CASH JOURNEY

One lifecycle. Seven connected processes.

The model joined commercial, operational, service and financial decisions. Select a stage to see what it required and where its dependencies crossed organizational boundaries.

STAGE 01 / ACQUIRE

Know the customer and the channel.

Lead, account, partner and opportunity management establish who the customer is, how the relationship is owned and what demand is forecast.

Business stakeholders
Sales, marketing, channel partners and regional commercial teams.
Handoff that matters
A trusted account and merchant identity must follow through quoting, contracting and finance.
Indicative platforms
Salesforce CRM and marketing/forecasting capabilities.

STAGE 02 / CONTRACT

Translate the promise into an executable agreement.

Approved configurations, prices, terms, service commitments and renewal conditions must be unambiguous before fulfillment starts.

Business stakeholders
Sales, commercial operations, Legal, product and Finance.
Handoff that matters
Contract lines and entitlements must match the products and recurring services that operations will activate and bill.
Indicative platforms
Salesforce, quoting/product catalog functions and related integrations.

STAGE 03 / ORDER

Accept a valid, fulfillable order once.

Order intake validates the offer, customer, quantities, delivery and billing instructions before transactions enter downstream systems.

Business stakeholders
Order operations, Sales, Finance and fulfillment partners.
Handoff that matters
A complete order needs correct legal entity, product, ship-to/bill-to and payment data without repeated manual entry.
Indicative platforms
Customer portals, Verifone Home/MOP and Oracle E-Business Suite order management.

STAGE 04 / ONBOARD

Make the merchant and service ready to transact.

Merchant validation, account creation, processor/gateway configuration, application setup and device or service provisioning bring the sold solution into usable operation.

Business stakeholders
Merchant operations, acquiring/processor partners, compliance and technical service teams.
Handoff that matters
Validated merchant and device identities must generate the right activation event and downstream service entitlement.
Indicative platforms
Onboarding APIs, Verifone Home/MOP, VHQ, IDS and regional provisioning platforms.

STAGE 05 / DELIVER

Complete physical and service delivery with traceability.

Prepare, ship, install, track, swap and repair devices, while managing assets, inventory and service readiness.

Business stakeholders
Supply chain, logistics, warehouses, field service and partners.
Handoff that matters
Shipment, serial number, installation and activation records must reconcile with customer contracts and billing triggers.
Indicative platforms
Oracle logistics and installed-base functions, field service and partner systems.

STAGE 06 / BILL & PAY

Invoice the right service and collect the cash.

Recurring and usage charges, invoice delivery, accounts receivable, payment settlement, direct debit, collections and reconciliation turn service delivery into financial results.

Business stakeholders
Finance, AR, billing operations, Treasury, country business owners and banking partners.
Handoff that matters
Contracts, activation dates, legal entity, tax, invoice recipients and payments must agree across systems.
Indicative platforms
Oracle EBS service billing, receivables and local financial/payment endpoints.

STAGE 07 / SUPPORT

Support what the customer actually owns and uses.

Case management, contact centers, entitlements, repairs, self-service and service history should provide a complete operational picture after activation.

Business stakeholders
Customer service, technical support, field service and customer success.
Handoff that matters
Support needs reliable account, asset, contract, activation and entitlement records, while complaints feed improvements upstream.
Indicative platforms
Salesforce Service Cloud, Amdocs and regional service platforms.
COMMON FOUNDATION

Customer and merchant identity · Product and contract master data · Legal entities · Service entitlements · Audit controls · Integrations · Reporting and accountability

The lifecycle is an operating-model map, not a claim that all seven stages operated on one implemented platform. Procure-to-Pay and Record-to-Report remained important adjacent financial and supply-chain dependencies.

03 THE CUSTOMER LENS

VOICE OF THE CUSTOMER

A process can look complete internally and still fail the customer.

I wanted the external journey to challenge assumptions made in internal workflow diagrams. A processed order is not a useful outcome if the merchant cannot transact, track delivery, resolve a problem or understand the invoice.

WHAT THE CUSTOMER EXPERIENCESWHAT THE BUSINESS MUST CONNECT AND TEST

“Can I order the right service once, with confidence?”

Offer and price clarity, complete intake, reliable tracking and ownership.

“When will it actually work?”

Merchant, processor, device and service provisioning through activation.

“Will the bill be correct?”

Contract terms, recurring and usage triggers, invoices and payment matching.

“Who can solve the problem?”

Entitlement visibility, first-contact resolution, complaints and handoffs.

HOW CUSTOMER FEEDBACK INFORMED THE APPROACH

Complaint themes, service contacts, satisfaction indicators, abandonment and first-call resolution were useful diagnostic inputs. A February 2019 Australia/New Zealand report provides a dated regional example of these signals, not a measured global improvement attributable to the transformation.

04 STRATEGY & OPERATING MODEL

FROM DIAGNOSIS TO BLUEPRINT

Standardize the business decisions, then design for legitimate exceptions.

The objective was a common process and data foundation across countries, legal entities, business units and channels, without pretending that all markets share the same statutory or commercial requirements.

01

Common process ownership

Define decisions and accountable owners across acquisition, ordering, fulfillment, activation, billing and service, instead of assigning only application owners.

02

Trusted master data

Align customer, merchant, product, service, contract and financial-entity definitions. Control the handoffs between system records.

03

Design by exception

Separate local tax, invoice, payment, language and regulatory obligations from inherited practices that no longer need to remain different.

04

Country-level reality

Use business workshops, integration tests, conversion rehearsals and local sign-off to challenge assumptions in the common template.

This describes the documented global strategy and operating-model direction. A formal worldwide design authority and some later governance enhancements appeared in a 2022 vendor proposal; their complete adoption is not established by the available records.

05 FINANCIAL LOGIC

THE VALUE CASE FOR FINANCE & INVESTORS

Measure the cash and customer result, not just the technical release.

For a CFO or investor, the value case connected faster activation, accurate service billing, more reliable collections, lower rework and better controls. These were economic hypotheses to prove, not automatic benefits of a software go-live.

REVENUE

Convert orders to active service

Complete and valid orders reduce delays between contract acceptance and billable activation.

CASH

Protect billing and collection

Correct recurring and usage charges, invoice delivery, AR and payment matching support timely collection.

ECONOMICS

Reduce friction and rework

Standard offerings, fewer handoffs and dependable self-service may lower cost per order and support contact.

CONTROL

Strengthen the record

Consistent master data, reconciliation and auditable exceptions make reporting and financial decisions more reliable.

How a value claim becomes defensible

Illustrative value-realization control sequence

  1. 01 Opportunity observed
  2. 02 Baseline established
  3. 03 Business case approved
  4. 04 Change implemented
  5. 05 Operating effect verified
  6. 06 Finance validates net value

Without an agreed baseline, period, owner, implementation cost, ongoing run-rate and Finance reconciliation, a proposed efficiency or cash benefit remains a target, not a realized result.

06 TECHNOLOGY & EXECUTION

TWO COMPLEMENTARY TRANSFORMATIONS

A stronger infrastructure foundation and a better business process are different investments.

Modern hosting can improve the durability of enterprise applications. It does not, by itself, fix an inaccurate contract, a broken service handoff or an invoice that never reaches the customer.

TRACK A / HOSTING FOUNDATION

Legacy on-premises infrastructure → Oracle Cloud Infrastructure

The modernization direction, as I recall it, was to move away from aging on-premises enterprise application hosting toward an Oracle Cloud Infrastructure IaaS model.

Business rationale: lifecycle management, resilience, refresh avoidance and a more sustainable hosting foundation.

Implementation extent, dates, migrated systems and measured cost or resilience benefits still require corroborating records.

TRACK B / BUSINESS & TRANSACTION PLATFORM

Process ownership → integrated operational systems

The business transformation drew on Salesforce CRM and service tools, Oracle E-Business Suite, Verifone Home/MOP, onboarding APIs, device and service platforms, integration services and analytics.

Business rationale: better transaction continuity, service activation, billing accuracy, customer experience and operational control.

The architecture evolved by market and date; it was not one universally deployed final system landscape.

THE CONNECTION

Both tracks depended on secure, reliable data exchange and accountable operating processes. An Oracle EBS application hosted on IaaS is not an Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP SaaS implementation.

07 AUTOMATION DEEP DIVE

MAVERICK / DIRECT-TO-MERCHANT

Automating the merchant journey meant standardizing what could be sold and activated.

High-volume Direct-to-Merchant orders could not scale by simply putting an old manual request form online. The design had to validate the offer, merchant and device before work moved downstream.

01AgreementOffer & terms
02ValidateMerchant & order
03AcceptClean transaction
04ProvisionDevice & service
05ActivateReady to transact
06Bill & collectFinancial continuity

PLAN A / PROPOSED AUTOMATED PATH

Integrated self-service and orchestration

The 2019 design described a new merchant experience with connected ordering, validation and provisioning across customer, order, device and financial systems.

A target design. Full production adoption and automation rates have not been verified.

PLAN B / PROPOSED ASSISTED FALLBACK

A controlled semi-automated route

When a market, product, acquiring arrangement or integration was not ready, existing forms and manual provisioning could preserve controlled execution.

A fallback in the documented roadmap, not evidence that every exception was resolved.

WHY THIS MATTERED

Incomplete merchant data, local configurations and unconnected entitlement or billing triggers would create downstream failure even if the first form was digital. The durable value lay in a reusable process, clear exceptions and reliable handoffs. The final Plan A/Plan B adoption mix remains unverified.

10 WHAT EXECUTION TAUGHT ME

LESSONS LEARNED

The harder lessons came after architecture diagrams looked finished.

The practical challenges were ownership, conversion quality, country readiness, financial validation and the customer experience after cutover. They shaped how I think about transformation beyond this program.

01

Harmonize before automation

Digitizing a poor process only moves defects faster. Agree the offering, rules and exceptions first.

02

Challenge local customization

Protect genuine statutory and market needs; challenge inherited differences that add support cost without value.

03

Treat data conversion as a business gate

Customer, contract, entitlement, pricing and payment data must be owned and reconciled before and after release.

04

Give business owners real capacity

Finance and country leaders must validate requirements and decisions. IT cannot substitute for their accountability.

05

Make authority explicit

Common templates, decision rights and a clearly accountable owner for global versus local design reduce drift.

06

Test the entire cash path

System integration testing and UAT must reach device readiness, invoice distribution, banking, returns and reconciliation.

07

Stage deployments to available capacity

Use pilots, manageable country waves, proven cutover criteria and an honest readiness decision.

08

Protect the customer after go-live

Hypercare, complaint handling, remediation and adoption are transformation work, not cleanup outside the project.

09

Close the loop with Finance

Track milestones through operational evidence to independently validated net benefit, not projected savings alone.

10

Coordinate, but separate, the tracks

Infrastructure hosting and process transformation should reinforce each other, while retaining distinct measures of success.

11 OUTCOMES & ENDURING IMPACT

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS

A more disciplined way to connect transformation work to real business results.

The available record establishes an enterprise-wide transformation vision, implementation activity in selected regions, a documented Sweden go-live and substantive remediation. It does not establish an independently validated global savings or completion figure.

DOCUMENTED DIRECTION

An end-to-end business model

A seven-stage customer, revenue and service lifecycle anchored work across functions, data and applications.

DOCUMENTED DELIVERY

Country implementation and stabilization

Sweden's May 2021 production milestone and subsequent fixes show both delivery and the operational accountability required after launch.

DOCUMENTED GOVERNANCE

Visible decisions and dependencies

BlackJack artifacts made changing dates, business readiness, risks and corrective actions inspectable at the executive level.

My enduring lesson is that enterprise value is created when commercial promises, operating execution, customer experience and financial controls become part of the same management conversation. Technology matters because it helps that conversation produce reliable results.
