Prostate research and guideline tracker

A curated overview of meaningful guideline, trial and regulatory developments and how to interpret them.

Editorial draft v2.0 · Evidence checked November 6, 2026 · Not clinically peer reviewed

Research is moving quickly, but not every headline changes clinical practice. This tracker highlights developments with identifiable sources and explains the population and evidence type behind each one.

Key distinction: Snapshot reviewed November 6, 2026. This page is curated, not a live newsfeed.

2026 screening and biopsy guidance

The AUA/SUO early detection amendment reviewed newer evidence on MRI, biomarkers and biopsy technique. EAU and Australian PCFA guidance also emphasize risk-adapted decisions, although exact screening ages and pathways differ by jurisdiction.

These are professional recommendations, not universal instructions. Use the detailed screening guide for regional comparisons, and consult current source text when discussing eligibility or intervals.

Supporting sources: AUA/SUO (2026), opens source in new tab · European Association of Urology (2026), opens source in new tab · Prostate Cancer Foundation of Australia (2026), opens source in new tab

New high-risk localized-cancer trial evidence

PROTEUS reported improved five-year metastasis-free survival with additional perioperative apalutamide on top of ADT in carefully selected men undergoing prostatectomy. Grade 3 or 4 adverse events were more frequent.

The published phase 3 study supports a specialist discussion, not routine use for all patients. Treatment indication and guideline adoption should be independently rechecked if a reader is considering care.

Supporting sources: New England Journal of Medicine (2026), opens source in new tab

Advanced disease regulatory development

On July 31, 2026, the U.S. FDA expanded the use of PSMA-targeted Pluvicto in combination with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor for a defined PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive population. This was a regulatory action supported by clinical trial evidence.

The drug is not a screening technique or localized-cancer therapy. It requires a qualifying disease state and PSMA imaging. Regulatory status and availability differ by country.

Supporting sources: U.S. FDA (2026), opens source in new tab

Promising research not yet routine care

Stockholm3 screening, experimental blood or urine tests, focal therapies, data-assisted surgery and telesurgery illustrate different degrees of investigation. Some have published clinical cohorts; others have only feasibility evidence or company plans.

We do not put preclinical mechanisms, observational associations, conference abstracts and FDA authorizations on the same evidence tier. Only developments that improve patients' understanding and have traceable support enter this public tracker.

Supporting sources: Annals of Internal Medicine (2026), opens source in new tab · NICE (2026), opens source in new tab · Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Is this a guideline, trial, approval or preliminary study?
  • What patients were actually studied?
  • Did the study improve a clinical outcome or only a test result?
  • Has regulatory or guideline status changed since publication?
