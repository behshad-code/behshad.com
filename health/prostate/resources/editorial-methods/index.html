How we evaluate evidence

Our standards for sources, study quality, medical claims, updates, transparency and privacy.

Updated November 6, 2026 · Men's health education · Not clinically peer reviewed

Men's Health Compass translates public medical evidence into useful explanations. The publication maintains responsibility for its editorial content, and its current guides have not received independent clinician peer review. AI tools can help organize research and draft explanations, but cannot provide clinical validation.

How we choose and assess information

We begin with professional guidelines, original peer-reviewed trials, official regulatory decisions and reputable institutional resources. We use news reporting to discover a story, not to establish a clinical claim. Research summaries are checked for study population, comparator, measured endpoint, follow-up, harms and limitations.

Different kinds of evidence answer different questions. A randomized trial may compare treatment strategies. A cohort may identify an association but cannot necessarily prove causation. A feasibility study or preprint cannot establish an approved standard of care. A manufacturer's roadmap is not a regulatory clearance.

Evidence labels and interpretation

  • Guideline: synthesis and recommendations for defined clinical situations and jurisdictions.
  • Regulatory decision: authorized products for stated indications, not universal clinical superiority.
  • Randomized trial: comparative evidence requiring careful attention to eligibility and adverse effects.
  • Systematic review: synthesis dependent on included studies and methods.
  • Observational evidence: associations that may be affected by selection and confounding.
  • Early research: preclinical, small pilot, conference or device feasibility findings that should not be treated as routine care.

Review dates, corrections and transparency

Each substantive page reports its evidence review date. Information may change after that date, so the original linked medical sources remain important. A change log records revisions. Before publication, proposed clinical claims and their links undergo an editorial evidence check; no professional peer review is claimed without a named qualified reviewer.

The v2 publication draws on an archived Flipboard discovery intake and separate institutional research compilations. The historical intake includes incomplete article-level access and is not published as a certified evidence database. Personal clinical records are not used as public patient examples.

Privacy and independence

The source filters and appointment-question builder work locally without uploading searches or selections. The site uses the existing privacy-conscious aggregate page-view analytics. No personal medical profiles are solicited or stored. Our privacy notice describes how aggregate page views and referrals are handled.

Editorial content is independent of advertising and manufacturer promotion. A company release may inform a technology update but must be identified as company information. This site is not affiliated with the medical organizations it links.
