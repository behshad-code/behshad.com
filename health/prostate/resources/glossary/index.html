Prostate health glossary

78 clear definitions to help readers understand medical terminology and research.

Updated November 6, 2026 · Independent educational writing · Not clinically peer reviewed

Plain-language definitions for the terms most commonly used in prostate testing, treatment, recovery and research. Definitions are contextual, not interpretations of your own medical results.

Active surveillance

Scheduled PSA, imaging, examination and possible repeat biopsies for appropriately selected cancer without immediate curative treatment.

Monitoring

Anastomosis

Surgical connection, such as the bladder being joined to the remaining urethra after prostatectomy.

Surgery

Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)

Treatment that lowers or suppresses the effects of male hormones that stimulate prostate cancer.

Treatment

Androgen receptor pathway inhibitor

Medicine that blocks androgen signaling through mechanisms distinct from testosterone suppression alone.

Treatment

Anterior approach

A route to the prostate through the tissues in front of the gland.

Surgery

Apalutamide

An androgen receptor inhibitor studied or used in defined prostate cancer treatment settings.

Treatment

Biochemical recurrence

A PSA pattern after treatment meeting criteria for concern about cancer recurrence.

Follow-up

Biomarker

A measurable characteristic used to estimate risk, disease status or treatment response.

Testing

Biopsy

Removal of tissue samples for examination under a microscope.

Diagnosis

Biparametric MRI

Prostate MRI protocol without the contrast-enhanced sequence of a multiparametric study.

Imaging

BPH

Benign prostatic hyperplasia: enlargement of the prostate that is not cancer.

Basics

Brachytherapy

Radiation delivered from sources placed in or near the prostate.

Radiation

BRCA2

A gene in which certain inherited variants can increase prostate cancer and other cancer risks.

Genetics

Cancer stage

Description of how far cancer has spread from its original location.

Diagnosis

Castration-resistant

Traditional term for cancer progressing despite very low testosterone levels.

Advanced cancer

Catheter

Tube used to drain urine, including after prostate surgery while healing occurs.

Recovery

Climacturia

Urine leakage during orgasm, which can occur after prostate surgery.

Recovery

Clinical risk group

Category combining PSA, grade, stage and other findings to inform decisions.

Diagnosis

Continence

Ability to control urine; research may define it as no pads or allowing a safety pad.

Recovery

Cryotherapy

Treatment using extreme cold to destroy tissue.

Treatment

Decipher

A tissue gene-expression classifier used in selected settings for prognosis.

Testing

Denonvilliers fascia

Tissue layer between the prostate and rectum involved in pelvic surgical anatomy.

Anatomy

Distant metastasis

Cancer spread to organs or bones away from its original site.

Advanced cancer

Dorsal venous complex

Veins near the front of the prostate that surgeons manage during removal.

Anatomy

Erectile function

Ability to achieve and maintain an erection, influenced by multiple nerves and vascular factors.

Recovery

External urinary sphincter

Muscle below the prostate that helps with voluntary urine control.

Anatomy

External-beam radiation

Treatment directed at cancer from an external radiation machine.

Radiation

FDA authorization

U.S. regulatory decision applying to a specified product and intended use.

Evidence

Focal therapy

An approach treating selected prostate tissue rather than the whole gland.

Treatment

Free PSA

The portion of PSA in blood not bound to certain proteins; may aid risk estimation.

Screening

Fusion biopsy

Biopsy technique using MRI targets overlaid on ultrasound guidance.

Diagnosis

Germline testing

Testing for inherited genetic variants potentially shared among family members.

Genetics

Gleason score

Pathology assessment of microscopic prostate cancer growth patterns.

Diagnosis

Grade Group

A five-level system derived from Gleason patterns describing cancer grade.

Diagnosis

High-risk localized cancer

Cancer confined to the pelvis with features indicating higher chance of spread or recurrence.

Treatment

Hormone-sensitive

Traditional term for cancer that remains responsive to androgen-pathway suppression.

Advanced cancer

Hypofractionation

Radiation delivered in fewer sessions with larger doses per session.

Radiation

Irreversible electroporation (IRE)

Focal technique using electrical pulses to damage targeted tissue.

Treatment

Metastasis-free survival

Time without cancer spread to distant sites or death, as defined by a study.

Research

Micro-ultrasound

High-resolution ultrasound under study or used in selected prostate assessment settings.

Imaging

MRI

Magnetic resonance imaging, used to examine prostate anatomy and suspicious lesions.

Imaging

Multiparametric MRI

Prostate MRI using multiple sequences, commonly including contrast-enhanced imaging.

Imaging

Nadir

Lowest PSA level reached after treatment, particularly relevant after radiation.

Follow-up

Nerve sparing

Surgical technique seeking to protect nerve networks involved in erections when cancer safety permits.

Surgery

NeuroSAFE

Intraoperative frozen-section examination near nerve bundles to help guide surgical margins.

Surgery

No-pad continence

Definition of urinary control with no protective pads.

Recovery

Overdiagnosis

Finding cancer that would not otherwise have caused symptoms or harm during a person's lifetime.

Screening

Pelvic-floor rehabilitation

Assessment and exercises or treatments aimed at improving urinary and pelvic function.

Recovery

Perineum

Area between the scrotum and anus, used as one route for prostate biopsy.

Diagnosis

PI-RADS

Standardized system for describing prostate MRI findings and suspicion.

Imaging

Positive surgical margin

Cancer cells present at the edge of a removed surgical specimen.

Surgery

Prostate apex

Lower tip of the prostate near the external urinary sphincter.

Anatomy

PSA

Prostate-specific antigen, a protein measured in blood that may increase for benign or cancerous reasons.

Screening

PSA density

PSA value divided by estimated prostate volume.

Screening

PSA doubling time

Estimated time for serial PSA values to double; interpretation depends on context and reliable measurements.

Follow-up

PSMA

Prostate-specific membrane antigen, a molecular target expressed by many prostate cancers.

Imaging

PSMA PET

Positron emission tomography with a PSMA-targeting tracer used for defined imaging indications.

Imaging

pT3 disease

Pathologic stage describing cancer extension beyond the prostate or into seminal vesicles.

Diagnosis

Radioligand therapy

Treatment using a radioactive molecule directed toward a target such as PSMA.

Advanced cancer

Randomized controlled trial

Study assigning participants to comparison groups by chance to reduce bias.

Evidence

Retzius-sparing

Posterior prostatectomy approach intended to preserve tissue in front of the bladder.

Surgery

Salvage treatment

Treatment after an earlier approach did not fully control cancer or recurrence occurred.

Follow-up

Seminal vesicles

Glands behind the bladder contributing much of the fluid in semen.

Anatomy

Single-port prostatectomy

Robotic operation using one principal access port rather than several.

Surgery

Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT)

Precise high-dose external radiation delivered in a small number of sessions.

Radiation

Systematic biopsy

Sampling prostate regions according to a pattern, not only MRI targets.

Diagnosis

Targeted biopsy

Sampling a suspicious area indicated by imaging or another localization method.

Diagnosis

Theranostics

Pairing molecular imaging of a target with a treatment directed at the same target.

Advanced cancer

Transperineal biopsy

Biopsy route through disinfected skin of the perineum.

Diagnosis

Transrectal biopsy

Biopsy route passing through the wall of the rectum.

Diagnosis

Transvesical approach

Surgical access through the bladder.

Surgery

Urethra

Tube that carries urine from the bladder out of the body.

Anatomy

Urinary sphincter

Muscle or muscles contributing to urinary control.

Anatomy

Very low risk

Older or version-specific category for certain minimal localized cancers; classifications can change.

Diagnosis

Vesicourethral connection

Reconstructed joining of bladder and urethra following prostatectomy.

Surgery

Watchful waiting

Symptom-focused follow-up, generally with a different treatment intent from active surveillance.

Monitoring

Watchful waiting versus surveillance

Comparison of symptom-led care and planned testing with intent to treat if risk changes.

Monitoring

Whole-gland treatment

Treatment directed at the entire prostate rather than a selected focal region.

Treatment
