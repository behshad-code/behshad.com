If prostate cancer returns or spreads

PSA recurrence, salvage options, metastatic treatments and how PSMA-targeted therapy fits.

Editorial draft v2.0 · Evidence checked November 6, 2026 · Not clinically peer reviewed

A recurrence after local treatment and cancer that has spread to distant sites are different situations. Terms such as biochemical recurrence, hormone-sensitive and resistant identify distinct decisions, not one uniform stage.

Key distinction: Approved drug indications vary by prior therapy, biomarker result and jurisdiction; verify them at point of care.

Biochemical recurrence is a signal, not a scan result

After prostatectomy, PSA is expected to become very low or undetectable. A confirmed rise can prompt evaluation for recurrence, sometimes before scans show a lesion. After radiation, PSA often remains measurable and recurrence is interpreted relative to its lowest value.

A treatment team considers PSA pattern, original pathology, interval since treatment and imaging findings. A negative PSMA PET does not prove microscopic disease is absent; waiting for a positive scan can sometimes affect salvage treatment opportunities.

Supporting sources: European Association of Urology (2026), opens source in new tab · UCSF/UCLA (2020), opens source in new tab

Salvage and local treatment discussions

Depending on the previous therapy and individual risk, selected patients may consider salvage radiotherapy, surgery, local procedures or systemic approaches. The risk of complications and likely benefit differs substantially depending on what treatment was delivered first.

The useful question is what the next intervention is intended to control and how its possible benefit compares with quality-of-life costs. Multidisciplinary consultation matters, especially when disease cannot be clearly localized.

Supporting sources: AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab · National Cancer Institute (2026), opens source in new tab

When cancer is metastatic

Metastatic prostate cancer may be treated with ADT, androgen receptor pathway drugs, chemotherapy, selected radiopharmaceuticals, other targeted agents or clinical trials. Which therapies apply depends on prior treatments, genetic features, imaging, symptoms and whether the cancer remains responsive to androgen-pathway suppression.

The evolving terminology in trial frameworks can differ from older patient documents. Ask the oncology team to describe the current disease state and planned sequence in plain language.

Supporting sources: European Association of Urology (2026), opens source in new tab · National Cancer Institute (2026), opens source in new tab

PSMA-targeted radioligand treatment

The FDA expanded Pluvicto's indication on July 31, 2026 for adults with PSMA-positive metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naïve or -sensitive prostate cancer, combined with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor. Patient selection requires appropriate PSMA imaging. Separate indications also exist for defined resistant metastatic settings.

The PSMAddition trial supported the new indication through improved radiographic progression-free survival, while overall-survival data were not mature at approval. This does not apply to a new localized diagnosis or mean that every person with a positive PSMA scan should receive the drug.

Supporting sources: U.S. FDA (2026), opens source in new tab · National Cancer Institute (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Is this a PSA-only recurrence or disease visible on imaging?
  • Would salvage therapy still be useful with a negative PET scan?
  • What treatments am I eligible for now versus later?
  • What is the evidence for the proposed sequence and its side effects?
