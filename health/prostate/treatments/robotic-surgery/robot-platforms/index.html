Surgical robot platforms compared

What da Vinci Xi, da Vinci 5, SP, Hugo and newer systems actually change.

Updated November 6, 2026 · Independent educational writing · Not clinically peer reviewed

Robot names describe instruments and workflows. The latest device does not automatically deliver better prostate cancer or recovery outcomes.

Key distinction: Regulatory approvals and company disclosures are not comparative patient-outcome trials.

The da Vinci family

Da Vinci Xi is an established multiport system. Da Vinci 5 adds updated ergonomics, sensing and force-feedback instruments, while da Vinci SP places camera and instruments through a single access device. These features can affect workflow and surgeon training.

Claims about greater computing power or reduced instrument force are product characteristics, not definitive randomized evidence of better cancer control, urinary continence or erectile recovery. The surgeon still directs the operation.

Additional robotic systems

Medtronic announced FDA clearance in December 2025 for its Hugo robotic-assisted system in urologic procedures including prostatectomy. Regulatory clearance establishes an authorized scope of device use; it does not rank patient outcomes versus other platforms.

Toumai and other systems have differing country-specific authorization and investigational status. Versius and SSI Mantra should be added to a regulatory comparison only after official device documentation and intended uses are independently verified.

Supporting sources: Medtronic (2025), opens source in new tab · Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab

What the patient can compare

Ask the surgeon about experience with the exact platform, annual volume, approach selection, complication management, cancer margins, urinary outcomes and erectile outcomes. Do not assume a hospital's technology purchase is a clinical recommendation.

Meaningful outcomes include how success is defined, baseline patient characteristics and follow-up duration. When no independently audited center-specific outcomes are available, that uncertainty should be acknowledged.

Supporting sources: AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab · Lancet Oncology (2018), opens source in new tab

Technology versus outcomes

Robot systems differ in arm design, port placement, camera capability, data ecosystem, force feedback, setup and training requirements. Most head-to-head clinical studies are not randomized, and selection and learning curves matter.

Before relying on a claim of superiority, check whether it reflects a device feature, a company report, an observational comparison or randomized patient outcomes. Recheck authorization by procedure and country.

Supporting sources: Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab · Medtronic (2025), opens source in new tab

How to compare devices without confusing features with patient results

Da Vinci Xi and da Vinci 5 are multiport robotic systems, while da Vinci SP is designed for single-port access. The systems differ in arm layout, instrument articulation, sensors, ergonomics and surgical workflow. Medtronic's Hugo system has received U.S. clearance for specified urologic procedures, but authorization must be checked for the particular model, date, country and use. Other platforms, including systems used internationally, may have different regulatory statuses.

Technical characteristics such as force feedback, computing capability or camera positioning can matter to operating teams. They do not establish superiority in margins, continence or erectile outcomes without appropriate comparative clinical research. Ask whether the surgeon has completed the learning curve on the exact platform, what outcomes are tracked using consistent definitions, and how the institution monitors complications or converts to another approach when needed.

Supporting sources: Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab · Medtronic (2025), opens source in new tab · Lancet Oncology (2018), opens source in new tab · AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Which robot and surgical approach will be used, and why?
  • How many cases has this team completed with the system?
  • What are its risk-adjusted cancer, continence and sexual-function outcomes?
  • Which claimed advantages are proven patient benefits?
