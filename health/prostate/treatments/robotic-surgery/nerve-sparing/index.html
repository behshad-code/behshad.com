Nerve sparing, continence and cancer margins

How nerve preservation, bladder support, Retzius-sparing and margin assessment influence the plan.

Updated November 9, 2026 · Men's health education · Not clinically peer reviewed

Technical refinements may protect urinary and sexual function, but must be weighed against the central objective of adequate cancer removal.

Key distinction: Functional benefits and cancer margins vary by patient selection, stage, surgeon and study design.

Nerve preservation is not a promise

Erectile nerve networks lie close to the prostate surface. Surgeons may preserve both sides, one side, part of a nerve bundle, or neither, depending on cancer location, stage and baseline function. The plan can change during surgery.

Preservation can improve the chances of functional recovery but cannot guarantee erections. Age, vascular health, previous function and later treatment are also important.

Supporting sources: Lancet Oncology (2025), opens source in new tab · National Cancer Institute (2026), opens source in new tab

Urinary control and measurement

The external urinary sphincter and surrounding support maintain continence. Bladder-neck preservation, urethral length, posterior reconstruction and pelvic-floor training may influence recovery. If bothersome leakage persists, specialist evaluation and rehabilitation options exist.

Continence reports differ according to whether they count patients as dry with zero pads, one safety pad, or based on questionnaire measures. Always request the definition and time point when comparing percentages.

Supporting sources: American Urological Association (2024), opens source in new tab · PLoS ONE (2022), opens source in new tab

Retzius-sparing and reconstruction

Retzius-sparing surgery approaches the prostate from behind to preserve structures in front of the bladder. Reviews suggest faster early continence for some groups, but long-term differences are often smaller, and margin findings may be a concern in particular higher-stage cases.

Other approaches may preserve the bladder neck or reconstruct tissue support. Cancer characteristics, anatomy and surgeon experience determine which techniques are appropriate.

Supporting sources: PLoS ONE (2022), opens source in new tab · European Association of Urology (2026), opens source in new tab

NeuroSAFE and margin checks

NeuroSAFE involves frozen-section assessment of tissue near the neurovascular bundles during surgery. The randomized NeuroSAFE PROOF study showed functional benefits in selected men undergoing robot-assisted prostatectomy.

Its relevance varies by center and patient. Margin safety must remain a central consideration even when a technique is intended to improve recovery.

Supporting sources: Lancet Oncology (2025), opens source in new tab · AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab

Balancing functional preservation with oncologic safety

Erectile nerves are adjacent to the prostate, and the safe plane for dissection depends on suspected cancer location and stage. Bilateral nerve sparing may be possible for some patients; unilateral or non-nerve-sparing surgery may be safer for others. Preserving more tissue is not automatically the right choice when the tumor may extend beyond the gland. A surgeon should explain the individual margin risk and how intraoperative findings could change the initial plan.

Continence-preserving approaches such as Retzius-sparing and bladder support reconstruction can influence early leakage in some studies, but technique, patient selection and definitions complicate comparisons. NeuroSAFE uses intraoperative frozen-section assessment in selected settings and has trial evidence, but it requires specialist workflow and is not universally available. Ask separately about measured cancer margins, long-term continence, erectile outcomes and recovery support, because no technique guarantees all four.

Supporting sources: Lancet Oncology (2025), opens source in new tab · PLoS ONE (2022), opens source in new tab · American Urological Association (2024), opens source in new tab · European Association of Urology (2026), opens source in new tab

For recovery after the nerve-preservation decision, visit the Prostate Surgery Recovery Compass and the sexual function recovery guide. Whether the surgeon can preserve nerves is not the same question as whether erections will return or respond to treatment.

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Why is nerve sparing planned or limited on each side?
  • Which continence and erectile-function definitions does the center use?
  • Is Retzius-sparing or reconstruction suitable for my cancer?
  • Is intraoperative margin assessment available or appropriate?
