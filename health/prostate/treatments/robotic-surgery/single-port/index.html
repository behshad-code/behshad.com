Single-port robotic prostatectomy

How the approaches differ, what SPARC found, and why observational outcomes need context.

Updated November 6, 2026 · Men's health education · Not clinically peer reviewed

Single-port systems pass a camera and instruments through one access point. That changes access, but does not make prostate removal a minor operation.

Key distinction: SPARC is a retrospective comparison and does not establish universal superiority.

Five surgical routes

Single-port approaches include transperitoneal, extraperitoneal, transvesical, transperineal and Retzius-sparing techniques. The transvesical route enters through the bladder; extraperitoneal access can avoid the main abdominal cavity. Each has different exposure and selection considerations.

One external incision does not determine cancer control. Internal dissection, nerve preservation, surgical margins and the team's experience matter more.

Supporting sources: European Urology Oncology (2026), opens source in new tab · National Cancer Institute (2026), opens source in new tab

The 2026 SPARC study

A multicentre SPARC analysis reviewed 2,230 single-port cases, including 1,699 extraperitoneal and 531 transvesical procedures. Researchers compared propensity-matched patients and reported differences in discharge patterns and earlier continence.

The study was retrospective, not randomized. Matching addresses some known differences but not every selection factor. Short follow-up cannot establish equal long-term cancer control.

Supporting sources: European Urology Oncology (2026), opens source in new tab

Interpreting recovery figures

Continence reports vary in definition, time point and baseline patient function. A finding of earlier pad-free recovery in a selected center does not predict an individual's outcome. Similarly, erectile results depend on baseline health and degree of nerve preservation.

The most useful comparison is the surgeon's risk-adjusted results for patients with similar risk and anatomy, alongside the published limitations.

Supporting sources: European Urology Oncology (2026), opens source in new tab · Lancet Oncology (2018), opens source in new tab

Questions about suitability

Ask why a particular approach offers a practical advantage for the individual case and what experience the surgeon has with that route. Past abdominal procedures may affect options but do not independently determine eligibility.

Also review complication rates, cancer margins by risk group, follow-up duration and what would happen if the planned access route needed to be changed.

Supporting sources: European Urology Oncology (2026), opens source in new tab · AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab

What single-port comparisons can actually demonstrate

A single-port platform enters through one access point, but the internal route can still differ substantially. Transvesical techniques access through the bladder; extraperitoneal approaches avoid entering the abdominal cavity; other routes require different positioning and dissection. The potential benefits and risks may differ with prior surgery, prostate size, anatomy and the surgeon's experience. Fewer skin incisions alone is not proof of better long-term function or cancer control.

The multicenter SPARC comparison included 2,230 cases and provides useful observational evidence about selected single-port approaches. Because the compared groups were not randomized, differences in case mix, surgeon experience and practice patterns may influence continence and recovery estimates. Compare the exact procedure, complication definitions, length of follow-up, pathology results and need for additional treatment rather than selecting an approach from an isolated percentage.

Supporting sources: European Urology Oncology (2026), opens source in new tab · Lancet Oncology (2018), opens source in new tab · AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Which single-port route is proposed and why?
  • What are the surgeon's outcomes for this exact technique?
  • Are recovery percentages pad-free or safety-pad definitions?
  • How long have the cancer outcomes been followed?
