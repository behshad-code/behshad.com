Surgical data, telesurgery and emerging automation

Separating current surgical assistance from company roadmaps and future remote operation.

Updated November 6, 2026 · Men's health education · Not clinically peer reviewed

Connected operating rooms and surgical data may improve training and collaboration. Development announcements, however, are not evidence that remote or partially automated prostatectomy has become routine care.

Key distinction: Company product roadmaps are not regulatory approvals or demonstrated clinical benefits.

Surgical data science

Robotic operating systems can capture video, instrument signals and workflow information. Researchers are examining whether these data can help training, identify performance variation and support quality improvement.

Measuring a task, recognizing an instrument on video or suggesting a movement is not equivalent to independently performing cancer surgery. Clinical validation and attention to privacy, bias and data governance remain necessary.

Telecollaboration is not telesurgery

Telecollaboration lets a remote expert observe or advise while the local team operates. Telesurgery involves remote control of instruments and raises additional requirements for latency, network resilience, security, emergency backup and licensing.

A feasibility demonstration should be clearly labeled research and not described as a treatment option available at ordinary medical centers. On-site clinical backup remains essential.

Supporting sources: Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab · Medtronic (2025), opens source in new tab

What manufacturers are planning

Intuitive has described telepresence as foundational for future telesurgery, along with development of automation-related features. The company's expectations for possible 2028 regulatory clearances are forward-looking statements and can change.

Force feedback, camera assistance and other tools may help surgeon workflows, but claims of improved long-term prostate cancer or functional outcomes need patient-level studies. Company timelines cannot be substituted for actual FDA decisions.

Supporting sources: Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions worth asking about new technology

Ask whether a proposed feature is currently authorized for the procedure and whether it is investigational. Understand who controls the operation, the role of any remote participant and how a technical failure would be managed.

Product news belongs in a dated research update section. Treatment comparisons should continue to rely on clinical outcomes, experienced surgical teams and patient-specific oncology priorities.

Supporting sources: AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab · Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab

Safety, connectivity and regulation matter as much as distance

Remote consultation, remote surgical mentoring and a surgeon directly controlling instruments from another location are different activities. True telesurgery introduces questions about network reliability, latency, failover, cybersecurity, licensing, training, emergency conversion and accountability for a patient on the operating table. Technical demonstrations do not show that a procedure is routinely authorized or improves patient outcomes.

Companies are exploring more connected surgical systems, telepresence and automation. Intuitive's 2026 disclosures describe prospective capabilities and expected regulatory milestones, not guarantees of clearance or demonstrated benefits. Before relying on a remote operation claim, ask which authority approved the exact clinical use, where the supervising and backup teams are located, what happens during loss of connectivity and what clinical outcomes have been independently published.

Supporting sources: Intuitive / SEC (2026), opens source in new tab · Medtronic (2025), opens source in new tab · AUA/ASTRO (2026), opens source in new tab

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Is this feature currently authorized for prostate surgery?
  • Is any part of the operation remote, and who has control?
  • How would a system or network interruption be handled?
  • What independently studied patient outcomes have improved?
