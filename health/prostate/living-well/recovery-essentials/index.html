Prostate Surgery Recovery Essentials

What to think through and what to have ready before surgery, so the first days and weeks at home are easier to navigate.

Published November 7, 2026 · Men's health education · Not clinically peer reviewed

Preparing for surgery involves more than the medical procedure. A little advance planning can help with everyday routines, especially if you're discharged with a urinary catheter or experience leakage after it's removed. The right supplies depend on your procedure, your surgeon's instructions and what your care team already provides.

Start with your care team. This guide is a preparation aid, not an individualized shopping list. Confirm catheter equipment, hygiene instructions, medicines, activity restrictions and supplies with your surgical team before buying anything. Some items may be provided at discharge.

Before surgery: a practical checklist

Use this list as a starting point for a conversation with your healthcare team and anyone helping you at home.

  • Ask what comes home with you. Confirm whether a catheter, drainage bags, securing supplies and written care instructions will be provided.
  • Plan your first few days. Arrange transportation, help with errands if needed, and a comfortable place to rest with essentials within reach.
  • Clarify the recovery restrictions. Ask about walking, lifting, driving, showering, work and the first follow-up appointment.
  • Know whom to call. Save the care team's daytime and after-hours numbers and ask which symptoms require urgent evaluation.
  • Prepare for potential leakage. Ask when protection might be useful after catheter removal and how to choose absorbency appropriate to your needs.

Practical supplies to consider

These are categories to discuss and plan for, not products everyone needs. Before purchasing, check what the hospital supplies and what works for your home.

Catheter-related supplies

Ask which collection bags, securing devices and cleaning materials your team recommends. Don't substitute equipment or change catheter care based on a shopping article.

Bed and chair protection

Washable or disposable absorbent underpads may be useful if leakage becomes an issue. Consider comfort, size and ease of cleaning.

Comfortable clothing

Loose, easy-to-change clothes may be more practical during early recovery. Follow your surgical team's instructions about dressings and incisions.

Home organization

Put water, instructions, a phone charger and commonly used essentials within easy reach. Plan a clear path for safe movement around the home.

Urinary protection after catheter removal

Urinary leakage can occur after prostatectomy, and recovery varies from person to person. Guards, pads and protective underwear offer different levels of coverage. Your care team can help you decide what type is suitable and when to use it. Read our urinary continence guide for more on recovery and treatment considerations.

Questions to ask before purchasing

  • Which catheter supplies will the hospital provide, and what should I purchase separately, if anything?
  • When might pads or protective underwear become useful, and what absorbency should I start with?
  • Are there any cleaning or hygiene products I should avoid while healing?
  • What is the best way to track and report urinary symptoms to my care team?

Quick answers

Should I buy catheter supplies before surgery?

Not necessarily. First ask your surgical team what you'll receive at discharge and whether they recommend any additional supplies. Use their instructions for catheter care.

Do I need maximum absorbency pads?

Not everyone does. Leakage varies, and absorbency, size and fit matter. Ask your care team what kind of protection is appropriate and reassess as your needs change.
