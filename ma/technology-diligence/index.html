01 / THE BUSINESS STAKES

An investment thesis meets a complex operating business

The diligence question wasn't simply whether the technology worked. It was whether the operating model could support the investment thesis.

The opportunity was a multibillion-dollar global take-private of an established payments technology company. Beneath the investment narrative was a collection of transaction services, regional businesses, inherited applications, support organizations and physical operations. The technology function connected nearly all of them.

That distinction mattered. Payments infrastructure had to remain available while the business evolved. A global order had to become a configured device, an activated service, a correct invoice and ultimately collected cash. Security and resilience were essential to customer trust. Fragmented financial and operational information could distort the economics of a proposed change.

I needed to evaluate how much of the future operating plan was realistic, what had to be protected immediately and which improvements required funding, business ownership and time.

THE INVESTOR QUESTION

What must we protect, what can we simplify, and what evidence would justify committing capital to each decision?

02 / MY ROLE

Independent technology diligence with an operating horizon

My assignment reached across the enterprise, but my accountability remained technology-led.

Before the close, I worked as a technology diligence consultant for the investor and as a prospective CIO. I examined corporate IT, business applications, cybersecurity, customer-facing payments infrastructure, enterprise data, technology vendors and the operational processes dependent on these systems.

The work involved reviewing incumbent plans and source documents, questioning technical and operational leaders and preparing observations that separated strengths, unresolved risks and potential opportunities. The wider deal team assessed strategy, finance and commercial operations. My role was to understand the technology implications of those activities, not to claim ownership of the acquisition decision.

After the transaction closed in 2018, I joined as CIO/CISO. That transition changed the standard of evidence: advisor observations had to become validated operating priorities, accountable workstreams and executable decisions. A proposed initiative was not yet an implemented result.

Two roles, one continuum

Before closing: challenge assumptions, document risks, test opportunities and prepare transition options.

After closing: inherit people and systems, protect continuity, validate funding and govern execution.

The first role is the focus of this story. Later operating achievements require their own records.

03 / HOW I APPROACHED IT

Follow the evidence, not just the management presentation

A reconstructed teaching framework showing how an investor question becomes a practical technology decision.

This is an editorial reconstruction informed by the 2018 record. It is not presented as a formally named consulting framework used at the time.

INVESTMENT QUESTION

Cybersecurity

Could the existing cybersecurity program protect customer trust, payment-service continuity and the investment thesis after ownership changed?

EVIDENCE TO REQUEST

Examine security leadership and accountability, independent assessment and penetration-test summaries, vulnerability remediation records, coverage of endpoint and infrastructure controls, privileged access, logging and monitoring practices, and tested incident escalation. Distinguish corporate security from customer-facing payments and cloud operations. Evidence should show where safeguards actually operate, which exceptions remain, and whether responsibilities extend to third parties and regional platforms. Review the effectiveness of controls rather than simply confirming that tools or policies exist.

DECISION AND IMPLICATION

Classify the difference between demonstrated controls, unsupported assurances and residual exposure. Significant risks belong in a prioritized, appropriately funded remediation plan with clear ownership, interim safeguards and measurable closure criteria. Neither an installed security product nor an announced remediation initiative should be counted as a completed risk reduction.

Across domains, my working logic was consistent: articulate the business consequence, request the evidence behind the claim, test the gaps with people responsible for operation, then distinguish an observed condition from a risk hypothesis or investable opportunity. A finding needed an owner, validation step and consequence for the first 100 days.

04 / CYBERSECURITY AND TRUST

Security spending is not security effectiveness

A previously reported public security incident made the strength and sustainability of protection, detection, response and recovery particularly material.

Management presented a security program with governance, tools and remediation activities. The diligence challenge was to distinguish the existence of those elements from evidence that controls were working across the relevant business and platform environments.

I examined the questions behind control coverage, independent testing, incident escalation, privileged access, third-party responsibilities, cloud operations and the effort required to sustain remediation. Corporate systems and customer-facing payment services could have different operational and security owners, making clear boundaries just as important as a common policy.

CONTROL OWNERSHIP

Who is accountable?

Know which teams own each material control, exception and incident decision across the operating estate.

OPERATING PROOF

Is it working?

Verify monitoring coverage, tested procedures, closure evidence and actual exceptions rather than counting products.

INVESTMENT DECISION

What remains?

Prioritize residual risk, compensating controls, sustainable capacity and realistic remediation funding.

Internal security findings and unverified historical incident allegations are intentionally excluded. This chapter does not assert that subsequent remediation was completed.

05 / CLOUD AND INFRASTRUCTURE

Two technology estates, different promises to keep

Live transaction platforms and enterprise IT shared some dependencies, but each had different business obligations.

PAYMENTS & CLOUD SERVICES

Customer platforms

Gateways, connected-device services, regional hosting, recovery and production monitoring.

SHARED DECISION LENSSecurity
Continuity
People
Economics
CORPORATE IT

Enterprise systems

Finance, CRM, identity, data, workplaces, infrastructure, vendors and operational support.

The payments environment needed continuous availability across markets and customer commitments. Corporate IT supported the application, network and information services needed by the broader business. Consolidation opportunities existed in both domains, but a simple inventory count was not a financially sound business case.

I evaluated the reliability of existing service operations and the dependencies behind proposed hosting, gateway and cloud simplification. Regulatory and contractual restrictions, country differences, disaster recovery, production expertise and migration costs could materially change the expected benefits.

The same logic applied to public cloud: moving workloads or adopting new services might improve agility while introducing requirements for monitoring, financial governance, response and tested recovery. A future-state diagram was a hypothesis until the operating design and economics were validated.

06 / SIGNATURE DEEP DIVE

Order to Cash: the business process beneath the software

To understand technology risk, follow the customer's journey from commercial promise to active service, a valid invoice and collected cash.

Order to Cash was a major diligence workstream, not a sidebar to ERP. The business combined hardware, software, payments infrastructure and recurring services, sold across channels and regional operating models. That complexity traveled from product configuration and contracts through onboarding, logistics, service activation, billing, collection and support.

The record revealed fragmented applications and country-specific practices, manual handoffs, inconsistent definitions and limited end-to-end visibility. Existing management teams had already developed portal, billing and integration proposals. My task was to assess their feasibility, implications and investment needs, not take credit for originating or implementing those plans.

INTERACTIVE VALUE CHAIN

01 / 06

Select & contract

Cross-functional handoff

What I needed to understand
Are product and service obligations captured consistently at the point of agreement?
Business consequence
Inconsistent definitions create exceptions and rework in every downstream step.

The real problem was ownership across boundaries

A CRM could record a sale while a financial system managed fulfillment, specialized platforms provisioned payments services, and service systems controlled customer entitlements. A local application improvement could still leave the customer waiting or Finance correcting an invoice.

Critical questions included who owns merchant and product identity, what event activates a billable service, whether contract changes flow into entitlements, how invoices reconcile, and whether customer-facing teams can see one reliable status. The proposed digital front office depended on common data, reusable integrations, business process governance, funding and market adoption.

For the transition plan, I viewed O2C as a cross-functional operating priority involving Sales, Finance, Services, technology and country leadership. Order-to-activate time, exception touches, invoice accuracy, collections and service quality were more meaningful outcomes to measure than merely deploying new software.

07 / SYSTEMS, DATA AND OPERATIONS

Global operations make technology decisions real

A system upgrade cannot be judged independently of the contracts, products, locations and people it serves.

FINANCE & BUSINESS DATA

Trust the numbers

Reconcile ledger and management reporting, cost definitions, data lineage and regional business rules before underwriting improvements.

SUPPLY CHAIN & REPAIR

Follow the product

Connect bills of material, demand, fulfillment, deployed devices, warranty, service entitlements and return operations.

CONTACT CENTERS

Protect the customer

Understand routing, vendor coverage, telephony, cases and service obligations before changing the support model.

The operating footprint included numerous legal entities, facilities and market-specific systems. Financial reporting and cost-of-goods reconciliation depended on technology and accounting rules. Supply chain planning, manufacturing, device deployment, returns and repair drew on multiple sources of product and operational data. Call centers relied on their own communications, service and outsourcing environments.

These were not claims that I directly managed Finance, manufacturing or every regional operation. They were technology dependencies material to the investor. A proposed rationalization could affect invoicing entities, inventory, customer service levels or continuity at a location many steps removed from a data center or application.

08 / RISK, VALUE AND INVESTMENT

An opportunity is not a realized benefit

The discipline of separating evidence from optimism protected the underwriting case and the future operating plan.

01ObservationWhat the record and review actually show
02HypothesisA potential risk or value opportunity
03EstimateModeled benefits, assumptions and costs
04ExecutionApproved owner, funding and completed change
05RealizationFinance-validated actual net outcome

Potential levers included platform rationalization, vendor and license optimization, support-model changes, better order processing, stronger data controls and reduced rework. The difficult part was estimating what remained after new cloud costs, transition work, contracts, people, investment and geographic constraints.

Different initiatives could overlap and double-count the same spend or headcount. Each needed a clear baseline, business owner, dependencies, operating metric, timing and benefit classification. Preliminary targets and tracking templates evidenced ambition and governance requirements, not delivered savings.

VALUE DISCIPLINE

Benefits belong in the investment case when they're evidenced and realistically funded. They become results only when executed and independently validated.

09 / FROM ADVISER TO CIO

The first 100 days: turn diligence into operating decisions

This is an evidence-informed reconstruction of recommended transition stages, not a record that every activity was completed by a specific date.

Pre-close diligence2018 management review, investor technology readout and transition preparation.
Transaction closesTransition from external technology consultant to CIO/CISO responsibilities.
Day 1 to Day 100Reconstructed governance and investment framework below; outcomes require post-close records.

DAY 1

Protect continuity

Verify incident leadership, privileged access, critical contracts, platform service coverage, change control and escalation paths.

Decision gate

Business-critical services and responsibilities are known and protected before high-risk change.

The inherited IT organization and incumbent modernization proposals provided an important starting point. My first responsibility after joining was not to assume every project or proposed synergy was right. It was to verify the baseline, understand capacity, protect the live business and define accountability.

Historical organization and business-case templates confirm that future-state design and investor reporting were contemplated. They do not, by themselves, establish the approved organization, milestones actually completed or quantified realized value. I would judge the first 100-day agenda by decision clarity, service continuity, credible priorities and reliable execution evidence.

10 / WHAT ENDURES

Technology diligence should become a usable operating agenda

The aim is not to collect the longest inventory. It's to leave the investment team and incoming leadership with decisions they can execute.

01

Follow the value chain

Order to Cash made the business process, and its owners, more important than any single software platform.

02

Test real effectiveness

Security, resilience and cloud readiness need evidence of operations, not only policies, products or design claims.

03

Price the transition

Organizational change, consolidation and modernization require skills, time, funding and customer protection.

04

Validate the realized result

The same Finance and operating discipline that underwrites savings must verify results after execution.

Moving from consultant to CIO/CISO gave me a different vantage point on the same questions. What matters most is whether diligence creates a responsible, actionable plan for the people who must run the business after close.
